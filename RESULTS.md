## Anything surprising
- **The coordinator isn't one long-lived session.** The coordinator that answered step 14b took over at 07:45:34 UTC on 2026-11-11, from a handoff that shows only its predecessor's chat posts. So it can't see what reached the old one. Anything a pipeline must remember has to live outside the coordinator: in the repo, on GitHub, or in project memory.
- **A thread's ordinary final reply reaches the coordinator as an event.** No special tool is needed for that (step 14b). `message_coordinator` is a second, earlier channel: a direct message while the thread is still running.
- **Threads have `create_trigger` and `fire_trigger`. The coordinator has neither** (C1, C2, C4). So a scheduled task in a project can be made only by a thread.
- **The coordinator answered 6 of the 7 reports we watched in run 2.** It answered C3, C4, 14c, the 08:09 pulse, the fired S2 fire run and C7, and it missed C2. The miss was yesterday, under the previous coordinator. Whether it answered the unattended 09:08 and 10:09 pulses wasn't checked.
- **Threads improvise when a step is refused** (16b: it fired without the text). Real skills must say what to do on a refusal.
- **A standalone routine starts inside the repo folder** (`cwd=/home/user/volumes-spike-2`). Project threads start in `/home/user`. That's probably why the workflow-by-name lookup depends on the kind of session.
- **A routine made at claude.ai/code/routines gets the account's connectors by default** (Claude-Docs, visualize).
- **The pulse doesn't reliably know how it was started.** It said "schedule" at 08:09 and 09:08, and "unknown" at 10:09.
